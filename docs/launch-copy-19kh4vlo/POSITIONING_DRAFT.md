# Focus Cycles — working positioning

Status: draft for Bud's review. Audience and conversion hypotheses below have not been customer-tested. This document does not replace approved app copy or change the product.

## Recommendation
A timer with a plan.

Focus Cycles is a Mac focus timer that pairs each work session with a target, a countdown, and a review.

Lead with Plan → Focus → Review. Let the calm visuals and Mac conveniences support that promise. Do not position it as an AI app, a habit tracker, a clinical attention treatment, or a guaranteed productivity improvement.

## Audience hypothesis
Mac users doing writing, study, creative work, or other self-directed tasks. Their likely tension: having time set aside without a concrete next action. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a reported customer finding.

## Distinction
The product pairs timing with planning and reflection. A target remains visible during focus. Session records remain available in History. This is a description of the product, not an unverified claim that competitors lack these features.

## Voice
Calm, direct, encouraging. Concrete tasks instead of hustle language. No invented origin story, claims of personal productivity gains, testimonials, or usage numbers.

## Evidence and exclusions
The current app and release checks support: Home/Session/History navigation, planning/review prompts, adjustable cycles and durations, Compact mode, menu bar timer, local sounds, scenic/custom backgrounds, light/dark appearance, history and CSV export, and recovery handling.

Do not carry forward older README claims such as “crash-proof,” “opens instantly,” “nothing is transmitted anywhere,” universal productivity gains, or public/open-source availability without a separate check. Do not describe CSV export as a full restorable backup. Weather is absent. Online Unsplash imagery still makes network requests. No claim about App Store privacy labels is made here.

The Work Cycles method attribution must remain clear; do not imply Ultraworking endorsement. Pricing in launch copy assumes the planned free release; verify the live listing before publishing. App Store copy intentionally omits a specific price.
